## The real shift

We often describe AI as a better search box: type a question, receive an answer. That description is convenient, but it misses what happens when a system can hold context, use tools, inspect intermediate results, and continue working over time.

The unit of value moves from the answer to the process. What matters is not only the final paragraph, but how the system framed the problem, what evidence it used, what it ignored, and whether it can recover when its first attempt fails.

## A collaborator needs a shared state

A useful collaborator does not merely wait for commands. It maintains a working model of the goal, the constraints, the decisions already made, and the open questions. This shared state makes longer work possible.

> The quality of collaboration depends less on how much the model says, and more on what both sides can keep consistent.

This is why context engineering, memory, and tool use belong together. They are different ways of preserving and changing the state of the work.

## Human judgment becomes more important

As execution becomes cheaper, deciding what deserves execution becomes more valuable. People must define quality, notice missing context, distinguish reversible experiments from consequential actions, and know when an apparently fluent result is not good enough.

I therefore prefer to think of AI not as an oracle, but as a collaborator whose speed is high, whose memory can be structured, and whose judgment must remain inspectable.
