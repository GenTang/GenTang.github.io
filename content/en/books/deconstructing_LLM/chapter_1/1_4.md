## 1.4 About This Book

Schopenhauer[^1] wrote in *The Wisdom of Life*:

> “To demonstrate intelligence in these disciplines—and the reputation for it is their hallmark—one must combine their materials in new ways. …”
>
> “Those who feel that they possess sound understanding and correct judgment, but do not believe that they truly possess the highest gifts of mind, should not be afraid to undertake tedious research and laborious work. …”
>
> “The merit of such a person’s discoveries may rest precisely on having overcome difficulties to obtain these materials. …”
>
> “This path to fame has another great advantage: it is easier to communicate what one has observed than what one has thought, just as it is easier to understand another person’s observations than another person’s thoughts.”

My original purpose in writing this book was not to pursue fame—although it would, of course, be disingenuous to deny that motive entirely. Rather, as Schopenhauer suggests, the book’s primary contribution lies in collecting material related to artificial intelligence and presenting it in a more elegant form. The material collected falls into two main categories. The first is theoretical explanations of models. Artificial intelligence spans disciplines including statistical analysis, machine learning, and econometrics. Different disciplines emphasize different aspects of the same model, and few resources integrate these perspectives into a detailed discussion. This book seeks to bridge the gaps between disciplines and uses analogies to reveal their connections. The second category is the engineering implementation of models—in other words, code. Third-party open-source tools provide mature, well-abstracted implementations of the classical models discussed in this book, and those implementations are not difficult to use. To support production use, however, these tools introduce so many layers of abstraction and implementation details that it becomes difficult to understand the core architecture of a model. This book therefore reimplements the essential parts of each model, using intuitive, clear code to help readers understand their structure more effectively.

Readers are encouraged not only to study the text, but also to examine the accompanying code[^2] in depth. Better still, they can use the implementations provided in this book as references and implement the models themselves; this may be the best way to learn. The mathematician Su Buqing once asked, “If you read a mathematics book without doing the exercises, how is that any different from reading a novel?” His point was not that mathematics books are easy to read—though perhaps they were for him—but that the essence of a discipline can be understood only through working out the calculations oneself. This principle applies to every field of science and engineering, and especially to artificial intelligence, which is highly practical. Frankly, this book is difficult to read because it deals with highly complex material. The great French mathematician Évariste Galois once said, “An author never harms his readers so much as when he conceals a difficulty.” Following this principle, the book discusses the key challenges in artificial intelligence in detail. Although this makes the book harder to read, it also helps readers better grasp the essence of the field.

In terms of content, the book is divided into three main parts, as described in detail in the preface. Readers are advised to proceed sequentially from beginning to end. Those who already have a foundation in machine learning and artificial intelligence may, however, choose to skip Chapters 3–5 and begin with the section on neural networks.


[^1]: Schopenhauer was a German philosopher and one of the leading representatives of pessimism. Although he completed his enduring masterpiece, *The World as Will and Representation*, at the age of thirty, it attracted almost no attention at the time. He received the recognition he deserved only gradually, late in life. This dramatic life experience nearly turned him into an optimist in his later years. He must have had a distinctive perspective on the subject of fame.
[^2]: Because space is limited, the main text includes only excerpts of the core code and omits most comments. For the complete code, see the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/GenTang/regression2chatgpt).
