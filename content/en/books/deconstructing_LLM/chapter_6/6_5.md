## 6.5 Chapter Summary

### 6.5.1 Key Takeaways

One of the central engineering problems in model implementation is how to estimate model parameters—that is, how to solve an optimization problem. Using PyTorch as its primary tool, this chapter examined gradient descent, a key optimization algorithm. Beginning from a preset initial point, the algorithm simulates a rolling ball and gradually approaches the minimum of the loss function. Although basic gradient descent is valuable for teaching, in practice it often converges inefficiently or becomes trapped at a local optimum. Researchers have introduced techniques such as stochastic gradients, momentum, and gradient normalization to improve optimization efficiency.

Selecting an optimization algorithm requires balancing many factors, including model complexity, dataset scale, training-time constraints, and the requirements of the final application. For small, relatively simple datasets and models, vanilla stochastic gradient descent may be sufficiently efficient. For large deep neural networks and tasks requiring rapid convergence, adaptive-learning-rate algorithms such as Adam may be more appropriate. The algorithm's computational cost and memory requirements must also be considered, along with its tendency to become trapped in local optima during training.

Because of space limitations, this chapter has not discussed these details. Interested readers may consult Andrew Ng's Stanford course “CS 229: Machine Learning” and Sebastian Ruder's paper “An Overview of Gradient Descent Optimization Algorithms.”
