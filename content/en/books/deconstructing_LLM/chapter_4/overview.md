# Chapter 4: Logistic Regression—The Latent Variable

> To be, or not to be: that is the question.
>
> — William Shakespeare

This chapter discusses the logistic regression model. The model has a profound influence on every aspect of daily life, from the advertisements displayed on a webpage to economic policies formulated at the national level, such as poverty-alleviation programs.

Logistic regression is not only widely used but also represents an important milestone in model development. Its construction embodies a crucial modeling idea: decompose a problem into a visible layer and an invisible layer, then combine a nonlinear transformation with a linear model to break an unfamiliar, complex problem down into familiar, simpler ones. It is no exaggeration to say that understanding the details of logistic regression means mastering the essence of model construction.

The logistic regression model itself has several important characteristics.

- **Simple model**: The modeling logic of logistic regression is clear and easy to grasp.
- **Broad applicability**: The model's assumptions are relatively easy to satisfy, so it applies to a wide range of settings. It is used extensively in fields such as medicine, finance, and marketing.
- **Strong interpretability**: The parameters of a logistic regression model have direct practical meaning, and their values directly reflect the importance of the features. This makes the model easier to interpret and helps explain how features affect predictions.
- **Reliable results**: Like linear regression, logistic regression has powerful analytical tools for assessing the stability of model parameters and results. Such analysis improves confidence in the model's conclusions.

The remainder of the chapter discusses these observations and explains why they hold.
