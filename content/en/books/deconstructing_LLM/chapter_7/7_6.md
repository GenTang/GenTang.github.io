## 7.6 Chapter Summary

### 7.6.1 Key Takeaways

This chapter examined and implemented a simplified backpropagation algorithm. Although simplified, its core design matches the open-source algorithm libraries used in industry: a computational-graph framework, the mathematical chain rule, and topological ordering on a directed acyclic graph.

When backpropagation trains a model, the memory cost of the computational graph is almost proportional to the number of observations used. The resulting graph expansion makes large-scale model training difficult. This chapter discussed three established solutions: gradient accumulation, mixed-precision training, and gradient checkpointing. Gradient accumulation and checkpointing trade computation for memory to reduce memory use without reducing training accuracy. Mixed-precision training lowers memory use by accepting some reduction in numerical precision.

Backpropagation is the cornerstone of neural networks, and many neural network training optimizations are fundamentally improvements to it. The chapter therefore also discussed parameter freezing and dropout as methods for improving model performance.

As neural networks continue to grow, traditional CPU computing can no longer meet their computational demands. GPU computing has become standard in production model training. Because a single machine has limited capacity, clusters and distributed computing are also commonly used to shorten training time.

Although this chapter covered a broad range of topics, its length and the scope of the book permitted only the central ideas to be introduced. Readers interested in further details can follow the pointers provided here and consult the official documentation and other materials for the relevant tools.
