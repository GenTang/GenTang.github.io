## 9.4 Chapter Summary

### 9.4.1 Key Takeaways

This chapter began using neural networks to solve relatively complex real-world problems. We first used a multilayer perceptron to recognize images. Building on the model discussion in [Chapter 8](/books/deconstructing_LLM/chapter-8), we constructed the model with PyTorch's packaged components and, in the process, examined in depth how to accelerate model training and address overfitting.

Multilayer perceptrons have several limitations when applied to complex image-recognition tasks, including their one-dimensional arrangement of neurons, fully connected architecture, and inability to transmit information across layers. Convolutional neural networks were introduced to overcome these limitations, marking the arrival of the deep-learning era. Convolutional neural networks introduced three key innovations: a three-dimensional arrangement of neurons; convolutional and pooling layers for capturing local features; and the widespread use of GPU computing. This chapter examined the first two innovations in detail and provided corresponding implementations. GPU computing was discussed in detail in [Section 7.5.1](/books/deconstructing_LLM/chapter-7/7-5#section-7-5-1).

Standard convolutional layers overcome the first two limitations of multilayer perceptrons, but predictive performance still encounters a bottleneck as network depth increases. Residual networks were introduced to address the difficulty of training deep networks. Residual connections make deep networks both feasible and efficient to train. Even neural networks with thousands of layers can be trained effectively, further broadening the applications of deep learning.
