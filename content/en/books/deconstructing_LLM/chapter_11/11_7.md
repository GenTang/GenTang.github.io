## 11.7 Chapter Summary

### 11.7.1 Key Takeaways

The attention mechanism is the core component of large language models and is considered the key to their understanding of human language. This chapter focused on that mechanism. It was originally developed to solve translation problems as an improvement to the classic encoder–decoder architecture. After further design refinements, attention became applicable not only to translation but also to tasks such as text-feature extraction and text generation, laying a solid foundation for the success of large language models.

Many models are based on attention, the best-known of which are GPT-style text-generation models. After introducing attention, this chapter therefore focused on the architecture and implementation of GPT-2. Once we constructed GPT-2 in code, we applied it to the Python-language-learning task mentioned repeatedly in preceding chapters. With relatively few parameters, GPT-2 substantially improved predictive performance over the earlier models.

Limited resources prevent us from pretraining a large language model from scratch, but many open-source base models are available within the LLM ecosystem. Because of how they are trained, base models can only predict the next token from the preceding text and thus have limited direct applications. The true appeal of large language models lies in the remarkable potential they reveal after fine-tuning. Following ChatGPT's successful example, supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, including reward modeling, can transform a model into a broadly useful intelligent assistant. After discussing techniques for using ChatGPT, the chapter therefore turned to model fine-tuning.

Fine-tuning differs little from ordinary model training and can be understood simply as better parameter initialization followed by partial training. Its theory is straightforward, but its applications are extremely broad. Depending on fine-tuning cost and application scenario, it can be divided into four modes, each with classic use cases. Efficiently fine-tuning an embedding model is of particular interest, and LoRA is the most successful representative technique. LoRA imposes few requirements on a model's architecture: the model need only contain linear components. Its adapters can also be installed and removed flexibly, leading to widespread adoption in practice. After covering the theory, we used LoRA to implement two instructive fine-tuning examples from ChatGPT: supervised fine-tuning and reward modeling.

The chapter also discussed the philosophical foundations of neural networks and their effects on social equality and the environment. Although these topics extend beyond technology, they are important to a holistic understanding of artificial intelligence. We hope they draw readers' attention to the ethical and social questions surrounding AI and encourage further reflection.
