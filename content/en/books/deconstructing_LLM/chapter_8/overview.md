# Chapter 8: Multilayer Perceptrons—The ‘Genesis’ of Neural Networks

> You can avoid reality, but you cannot avoid the consequences of avoiding reality.
>
> — Ayn Rand

In artificial intelligence, neural networks shine like a star that never dims. Over the past decade, we have witnessed astonishing advances: from convolutional neural networks, which demonstrated the potential of deep learning, to AlphaGo, which defeated human Go champions, and then to the celebrated ChatGPT. Neural networks have continued to surprise the world. Yet alongside this excitement, they have also brought us greater confusion, unease, and awe.

The idea of neural networks can be traced to bionics[^1]: using computers and mathematical models to simulate the human nervous system. Our understanding of the models themselves, however, remains limited, and their operating principles still seem shrouded in mist. We know little not only about how they work, but also about the limits of what they may ultimately achieve.

Academia and industry are sharply divided over the prospects of neural networks. One camp believes that the current enthusiasm for artificial intelligence may merely be a bubble and that the field has yet to make a substantive breakthrough. The distinguished scholar Professor Michael I. Jordan holds this view, arguing that AI remains far from human-level intelligence. Although neural networks can “simulate” intelligence in certain domains, rigorous analysis does not make that equivalent to genuine intelligence.

Another camp firmly believes that AI is on the verge of a breakthrough and will bring enormous benefits to humanity. Entrepreneurs such as Mark Zuckerberg and Ma Huateng hold this view. They are confident about AI's prospects and believe that, like electricity, it will bring about a new industrial revolution that transforms industries and human life.

Many others, of course, worry that AI may introduce tremendous risks, because we are creating a new type of intelligent agent that is, in some sense, nearly immortal—a form of “silicon-based life.” Elon Musk in industry and Geoffrey Hinton in academia represent this view. Although today's AI has not yet posed a direct threat, its rapid development may expose us to potential risks in the future. Those risks need not resemble the scenarios portrayed in science-fiction films; they may instead take such concrete forms as large-scale unemployment.

Whatever position one takes, the future has already arrived. Faced with artificial intelligence, one may admire, quote, refute, question, praise, or criticize it, but one cannot ignore its existence. Understanding and mastering neural networks has become an essential skill for a new generation of data scientists.

With the foundations laid in the preceding chapters, we now begin an in-depth exploration of neural-network models. We start with the most fundamental model, the multilayer perceptron (MLP), then examine in detail the convolutional neural network (CNN), which demonstrated the potential of deep learning. Next come recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for sequential data, followed eventually by a detailed discussion of how to train and build today's most advanced conversational AI models, such as ChatGPT.

[^1]: Bionics is the science of imitating the special capabilities of living organisms. It develops new machines and technologies by studying the principles of biological structures and functions. This description is adapted from Wikipedia.
