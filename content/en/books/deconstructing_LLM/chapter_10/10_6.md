## 10.6 Chapter Summary

### 10.6.1 Key Takeaways

Language is both the dwelling place of human intelligence and an extraordinarily complex domain that is difficult to model. Beginning with this chapter, the book turns its attention to natural language processing. We first examined how language is digitized, a process involving two key techniques: tokenization and text embedding. Tokenization has long been a challenge for Chinese and directly affects how well models can learn the language.

Natural language processing encompasses many tasks. To improve model performance on language-processing tasks, researchers developed transfer learning. Transfer learning consists of two stages: pretraining and fine-tuning. Three common pretraining modes are autoregressive, autoencoding, and sequence-to-sequence. In light of current industry trends, this chapter restricted its discussion to the autoregressive mode.

In the autoregressive setting, this chapter used a multilayer perceptron, a standard recurrent neural network, and a long short-term memory network to learn from open-source Python code. Because of its architectural limitations, a multilayer perceptron is best suited to fixed-length inputs and has a relatively weak ability to model sequential data. Recurrent neural networks can accept variable-length inputs and produce either fixed- or variable-length outputs, making them highly effective in natural language processing. Standard recurrent neural networks, however, are constrained by short-term memory and struggle to capture long-range dependencies. Long short-term memory networks were introduced to address this problem. Their cell states and gating mechanisms support the effective transmission of information across long distances, improving predictive accuracy.

Structurally, the neural networks discussed earlier generally organized neurons in a hierarchy of layers, with no connections between neurons in the same layer, and the neurons themselves were relatively simple. Recurrent neural networks break both limitations, giving neural networks more flexible structures that can capture complex relationships within data more effectively. Recurrent neural networks also have many sophisticated variants, including multilayer, bidirectional, encoder, and decoder structures. Because of space constraints, this chapter introduced them only briefly; interested readers can explore them further in other literature.

As their name suggests, recurrence is the defining characteristic of recurrent neural networks. The serial nature of recurrent computation substantially limits computational efficiency. Although tensor operations can accelerate model training, the architectural limitations mean that serial computation cannot be eliminated entirely. Consequently, both models implemented in this chapter—the standard recurrent neural network and the long short-term memory network—require considerable computation time on large datasets.
