## 2.4 Chapter Summary

This chapter has briefly surveyed mathematical tools commonly used in artificial intelligence, laying a theoretical foundation for the models introduced later. As stated at the outset, the purpose is to familiarize readers with the mathematical concepts and notation that appear frequently in subsequent chapters. Proofs are therefore sketched only briefly, and the rigorous formulations of some theorems are not developed in detail. In addition, more advanced mathematical tools, such as convex optimization, were omitted because of space limitations. Of course, covering all of this material in full would turn this book into several mathematics books “that make you want to stop reading after the first page.”[^yang]

Readers interested in the mathematical details are encouraged to consult specialized mathematics books, such as *The Matrix Cookbook* by Kaare Brandt Petersen and Michael Syskind Pedersen, *Mathematics for Computer Science* by Eric Lehman, *Calculus* by Gilbert Strang, and a book by Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe.

[^yang]: This is a comment on mathematics books by the physicist Chen-Ning Yang. His original remark was: “There are two kinds of mathematics books: those that become impossible to continue after the first chapter, and those that become impossible to continue after the first page.” The author, however, completely disagrees with this view.
